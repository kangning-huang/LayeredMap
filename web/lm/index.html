A map app for the curious

Open a map file. See what it says.

NimbleMap is for people who love maps but have never used GIS. Double-click a file of places, tracks or climate data, and it appears on the map. Click a place or a feature to see what makes it interesting: the strongest storm, the largest walled city, the hottest week.

Why it's different

What if GIS were as easy to use as an Apple app?

QGIS and ArcGIS are powerful tools for working with spatial data. But for someone opening them for the first time, even making a simple map can be difficult. Hundreds of tools and settings stand between the user and the map. What would GIS look like if it were designed by the people who made the iPhone? Which tools would they keep, which would they hide, and which would they remove altogether? NimbleMap explores this idea. It brings the useful parts of GIS into a map app that anyone curious about places can use.

Answers before tools

Most GIS software starts with tools. NimbleMap starts with questions. Click something on the map, and it tells you what it is and how it compares with the rest of its layer.

Honest numbers

Every number comes from your data, not just what happens to be visible on the screen. If some records cannot be placed on the map, NimbleMap tells you how many and why. Your original files remain unchanged.

Try anything

You can undo every step. Every result also comes with an explanation, in plain words, of how it was produced.

NimbleMap is an independent project, not affiliated with Apple, Esri or the QGIS project.

How it works

Layers, like tracing paper

A map in NimbleMap consists of layers stacked over a background map, much like sheets of tracing paper. Open a file, and it becomes a new layer. There is no setup wizard, no project to create and no empty toolbox to navigate.

Open

Drag in a CSV with coordinates, a GeoJSON, a Shapefile, a GeoPackage, a GeoTIFF or a NetCDF climate file. If NimbleMap needs to make an assumption, such as which columns contain coordinates, it tells you what it assumed and lets you change it.

Click

Click a city, a storm or a single cell in a climate grid. NimbleMap first shows you what it is and how it compares with the rest of its layer: “highest of 4,999” or “lower than 80% of the others”. Relevant tools appear when you need them.

Share

Colour your map by a value, filter the data and export the result as an image. Add a title, legend, scale bar and credits. Choose a size that fits a presentation slide, an A4 page or a square post.

Built-in examples

Things an everyday map app can't show you

NimbleMap comes with eight example sets, available with one click under Examples. Here are a few:

Typhoons and hurricanes

4,999 storm tracks since 1980, covering every ocean, from NOAA’s IBTrACS archive. Find Haiyan, colour the tracks by wind speed and see where the strongest storms occur.

IBTrACS v04r01 · 1980–2026

Walled cities of China, 1866

1,789 city walls from late imperial China, including the capital, county towns and Manchu garrisons. See where these cities stood, drawn over satellite imagery.

Xue et al. 2021 · CC BY 4.0

Heatwaves, summer 2023

Daily heatwave intensity across the world from June to August 2023, stored in a NetCDF climate file. Move the time slider, or press Play to see how heatwaves developed and moved through the summer.

Copernicus GDO (JRC) · CC BY 4.0

Also included: big earthquakes since 1900, volcanoes, airports, power plants, and world cities, countries and elevation.

Free and Pro

Free to look. Pro to ask.

The free version lets you open, overlay, style and share spatial data. NimbleMap Pro adds an assistant that lets you ask questions about your map layers in plain words.

Free

Free download

  • Opens CSV, Excel, GeoJSON, Shapefile, GeoPackage, KML, GPX, GeoTIFF and NetCDF
  • Click anything to see its information and how it compares with other features
  • Colour by a value, filter, compare and label
  • Join a spreadsheet to places using a shared column
  • Time slider for climate grids
  • Light, dark and satellite basemaps; works offline
  • Supports China's map coordinates (GCJ-02, BD-09)
  • Exports map images and data

Pro PRO

Everything in Free, plus:

  • The Ask box: ask questions about your layers in English or Chinese, by typing or speaking
  • See answers as previews on the map, with a step-by-step record you can check. No guessing.
  • Built-in world layers: countries, provinces, cities, rivers, lakes and elevation. Ask questions such as “Which country has the most power plants?”
  • Analysis buttons appear on cards when relevant, such as “Show an area within a distance”
  • Your data stays on your Mac. Only layer names, fields and extents are sent, never the values.

Get NimbleMap
