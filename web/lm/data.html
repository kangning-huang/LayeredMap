Data to explore

Where can I find map data?

Start with the map data you already have, then try some of the best free public datasets. Each one opens in NimbleMap, and each comes with a line on what to look for.

NimbleMap doesn't host these files. Each link goes to the original source, so you always get the latest version and the publisher's own terms. Links were checked on 20 October 2026.

Start here

Map data you already have

Your phone, your watch and your spreadsheets are full of places.

Runs, rides and hikes (GPX)

Strava: open an activity, then ⋯ › Export GPX (how). Garmin Connect on the web: open an activity, the gear icon › Export to GPX (how). Apple Health: your picture › Export All Health Data (how); unzip it and the workout-routes folder holds one GPX per outdoor workout. Drag a whole folder of GPX files into NimbleMap at once.

Your Google Maps places

Google Takeout › Maps (your places) gives a Saved Places.json file, which is GeoJSON and opens as points. Your Timeline (location history) now lives on your phone, and its export is a plain JSON file, not a map format NimbleMap opens yet.

A spreadsheet of places

Any CSV, TSV or Excel file with latitude and longitude columns opens as points. NimbleMap finds columns named like lat/latitude and lon/lng/longitude and tells you which it picked; if your columns have other names, choose them yourself. A table with only place names, such as countries, US states or Chinese provinces, can be joined to boundaries instead.

How to open a download Download the file, then drag it into NimbleMap. Shapefiles, GeoPackages and NetCDF files also open with a double-click; for other files, right-click › Open With › NimbleMap. If it arrives as a .zip, double-click it in Finder to unzip first (a zipped Shapefile can also be opened as it is). A .gz file unpacks the same way. If a file opens as text in your browser instead of downloading, press ⌘S to save it.

Earth & hazards

Earthquakes, fires and tornadoes, as they happen and over decades.

Earthquakes, live

USGS Earthquake Hazards Program

  • CSV
  • about 10,000 rows for 30 days
  • Public domain

Every earthquake recorded in the past hour, day, week or month, updated every minute. Colour by mag and you'll see how many small quakes happen that never make the news.

Fires burning right now

NASA FIRMS

  • CSV · Shapefile · KML
  • about 11,000 rows per day (MODIS)
  • NASA open data

Every fire hotspot satellites detected in the last 24 hours, 48 hours or 7 days. Colour by frp (fire power) to find the fiercest, or filter daynight to compare day and night.

73,458 tornado paths from 1950 to 2025, each with its strength, injuries, deaths, length and width. Colour by mag and Tornado Alley draws itself. Hail and wind reports are on the same page.

Climate & oceans

How much warmer or cooler each 2° square of the planet was each month than the 1951–1980 average. Double-click the .gz to unpack it, then play the time slider and watch the colours turn.

Licences and credit

Each dataset belongs to its publisher, and the licence on each card is the one the source states. Public domain and CC0 data can be used freely. CC BY, ODbL and similar licences ask you to credit the source when you share a map, for example “Data: USGS” or “© OpenStreetMap contributors”. NC licences rule out commercial use. When in doubt, read the terms on the source page.
