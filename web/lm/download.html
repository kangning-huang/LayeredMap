Free for Mac and Windows

Open your map files on Mac or Windows. They just line up.

Drag a Shapefile, a GeoJSON, a CSV with latitude and longitude, or a GeoTIFF into NimbleMap, and it is on a map. Add another and it stacks in the right place. There is no coordinate system to set and no project to create.

When you just want to look

A map file, open in a second, not a GIS to learn

Most people with a shapefile don't need a full GIS. They need to see what is in it, check it lines up with something else, and maybe send a picture of it.

Drag it in, or double-click

Drag any file it reads into NimbleMap, or onto its icon in the Dock. Shapefile, GeoPackage, NetCDF, KML and GPX files also open with a double-click in Finder, unless another map app has claimed them. CSV, Excel, GeoTIFF, GeoJSON and zip files open in other apps when double-clicked: right-click › Open With › NimbleMap instead.

Layers line up on their own

A shapefile's .prj is read and its coordinates converted, so CSV points land on top of district boundaries instead of somewhere off the map. If a file's coordinate system can't be read, the app says so in one line.

CSV points in one drop

Drag a spreadsheet with latitude and longitude columns into NimbleMap. The app finds the columns, puts every row on the map, and tells you which columns it picked so you can change them.

GeoTIFFs on a real map

Preview shows a GeoTIFF as a flat picture. Here a satellite scene, a drone survey or an elevation model is placed where it belongs, under your other layers.

Your files stay on your computer

Nothing is uploaded. The free app opens files locally and works offline, which matters for survey data, client files and anything sensitive.

China's map coordinates

Points from Amap, Tencent or Baidu Maps use China's shifted GCJ-02 or BD-09 coordinates. When a layer in China looks like one of these, the app says so and converts it with one click, so it lines up with the basemap and your other data.

Formats

What it opens

Shapefile (.shp)

Keep the .shp with its .dbf, .shx and .prj beside it, or open the .zip they came in. Attributes show in a table and on a card when you click a shape. Older files in Chinese or other encodings are read with their .cpg.

GeoJSON

Points, lines and polygons, with every property in the table.

CSV and Excel

Any table with latitude and longitude columns, or a WKT geometry column. A table without coordinates can be joined to places by a shared column, such as a country or district name.

GeoTIFF

Elevation, temperature, land cover, satellite and drone imagery. Click a cell to read its value; colour it by value with a legend.

NetCDF

Climate and weather grids, with a time slider to step or play through the days.

Also

GeoPackage, KML and KMZ from Google Earth, and GPX tracks from a phone or watch.

For people who know GIS

What if GIS were as easy to use as an Apple app?

QGIS and ArcGIS are powerful tools for working with spatial data. But for someone opening them for the first time, even making a simple map can be difficult. Hundreds of tools and settings stand between the user and the map. What would GIS look like if it were designed by the people who made the iPhone? Which tools would they keep, which would they hide, and which would they remove altogether? NimbleMap explores this idea. It brings the useful parts of GIS into a map app that anyone curious about places can use.

Answers before tools

Most GIS software starts with tools. NimbleMap starts with questions. Click something on the map, and it tells you what it is and how it compares with the rest of its layer.

Honest numbers

Every number comes from your data, not just what happens to be visible on the screen. If some records cannot be placed on the map, NimbleMap tells you how many and why. Your original files remain unchanged.

Try anything

You can undo every step. Every result also comes with an explanation, in plain words, of how it was produced.

Honest comparison

NimbleMap or QGIS?

QGIS is the free, full GIS, and a very good one. NimbleMap is for the times it is more than you need.

NimbleMap is enough when you want to

  • See what is in a file and where it is
  • Check that two layers line up
  • Colour, filter and label by a value
  • Join a spreadsheet to places by name
  • Run a quick analysis from a card: an area within a distance, points counted in each area, the part of a layer inside another
  • Export a finished map image for a slide or report

Use QGIS when you need to

  • Edit shapes or attributes
  • Georeference a scanned map
  • Dissolve, chain geoprocessing tools into models, or run advanced analysis
  • Choose or define a map projection yourself
  • Build a multi-page print layout or use plugins

Browser viewers are quicker still for a one-off look, but they need the file uploaded or held in a browser tab. NimbleMap keeps files local and handles large ones.

Questions

Common questions

Is there free software to open a shapefile on Mac or Windows?

Yes. NimbleMap is free. Double-click the .shp in Finder, or drag it or its .zip into NimbleMap. Keep the .dbf, .shx and .prj files next to it, since a shapefile is really a set of files.

Why don't my CSV points show up on top of my shapefile?

Usually the shapefile is stored in a projected coordinate system (metres from some origin) while the CSV holds latitude and longitude, so the two land in different places. NimbleMap reads the shapefile's .prj and converts it to latitude and longitude, so both line up. If the .prj is missing or not understood, it says so instead of guessing silently.

Why does Preview open my GeoTIFF but not on a map?

Preview and Photos treat a GeoTIFF as an ordinary image and ignore the location stored inside it. NimbleMap reads that location and draws the image in place, under or over your other layers, and shows the value of any cell you click.

Do I need to know GIS or coordinate systems?

No. Files open without settings, and when the app has to guess something, such as which columns hold the coordinates, it tells you in one line and lets you change it. The glossary explains map words in plain language.

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

Not in the free app: everything is read, analysed and drawn on your computer, and it works offline. Basemap tiles are downloaded when you're online, but your data is never sent. In Pro, the Ask box sends the assistant your layer names, field names and extents so it can understand the question, never the values.
